# Writing the outreach message

The message has one job: make the reader want to look at the candidate's
application. It is not a cover letter.

## Principles

- **Specific beats polite.** Name the role and link it. Name one thing about the
  team or the person's public work that is genuinely relevant. Generic praise
  ("I love your company's mission") reads like a template.
- **Lead with value.** Within the first two sentences, give one concrete result
  from the candidate's background that matches a key requirement. Use numbers if
  the candidate gave them. Never invent numbers or achievements.
- **One clear request.** Pick one: "Could you take a look at my application?", "Is
  this role still a match for someone with X?", or "Would a 15-minute call make
  sense?" Do not stack several requests.
- **Honest.** Say "I applied today" only if it is true. If the candidate has not
  applied yet, write "I'm applying today" and remind them to do it.
- **Short.** A DM is 3–5 sentences. An email is up to about 150 words.
- **No pressure, no flattery.** Do not mention the ATS or AI filters. Do not
  complain about hiring processes. Do not use urgency tricks.
- **Respect the reader's context.** Mention a talk or a post only if it relates to
  the role. Never mention anything personal.
- **The candidate's voice.** Plain, direct sentences. Avoid buzzwords such as
  "passionate", "rockstar", "synergy", and "leverage". If the candidate wrote
  something themselves, match its tone.

## Structure

1. **Hook (1 sentence):** why you are writing to this person, about this role.
2. **Value (1–2 sentences):** the strongest match between the candidate and the
   role.
3. **Genuine interest (1 sentence):** a specific reason this team or problem
   interests the candidate.
4. **Request (1 sentence):** one small, easy request.
5. **Sign-off:** name, and one link (portfolio, GitHub, or personal site).

## Email subject line

Make it specific and short, for example:

- `<Role> — <one strong match in 4–6 words>`
- `Re: your post about the <team> role`

## Example (email, about 110 words)

> Subject: Senior Backend Engineer — Go, Kafka, 5 years on payments infra
>
> Hi Sam,
>
> I saw your post that the Platform team is hiring a Senior Backend Engineer, and I
> applied today. For the past five years I have built Go services on Kafka that
> process card payments, and I cut p99 latency (the response time for the slowest
> 1% of requests) on our ledger service from 800 ms to 120 ms.
>
> Your talk on moving the workflow engine to event sourcing is close to what I want
> to work on next.
>
> Would you be open to taking a look at my application?
>
> Thanks,
> Alex
> alex.dev

## Follow-up (after about 7 working days, once)

Two or three sentences. Add one new piece of information, such as a relevant
project or an article. Repeat the request in a lighter form. Do not send a third
message.

## Placeholders

If the candidate's background is missing, use clear placeholders in square
brackets. Examples: `[your strongest result in distributed systems]`,
`[link to your GitHub]`. Do not fill them with invented details.
