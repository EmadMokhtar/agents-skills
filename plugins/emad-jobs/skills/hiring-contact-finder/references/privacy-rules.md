# Privacy rules

These rules apply every time the skill runs. They exist for two reasons:

1. The people you look up did not ask to be researched. They deserve the same
   respect a careful professional would show.
2. Outreach that feels invasive fails. A recruiter who receives a message that
   shows the sender dug into their private life will not reply. They may also
   flag the candidate.

## Allowed

- Name, job title, team, and employer, as shown in public professional sources.
- Things the person published in a work context: job posts, talks, blog posts,
  open-source work, podcasts, company pages.
- Professional contact channels:
  - a contact the person published for this purpose
  - a reply or DM on their public post about the job
  - a public professional website or its contact form
  - a company recruiting address
  - a work email (published, or guessed from the company's public format and
    marked unverified)

## Not allowed

- Personal phone numbers, home or postal addresses, and personal email addresses.
- Family members, relationships, health, religion, politics, or other private
  details.
- Private or clearly personal social media accounts. A public account that the
  person uses for work posts, and where they posted about the job, is fine.
- LinkedIn. The user chose to keep it out of this skill.
- People-search websites, data brokers, contact-scraping tools that sell personal
  data, leaked databases, or anything behind a login you do not own.
- Tricks to reveal hidden information, such as guessing private URLs, testing
  password-reset pages, or probing mail servers to confirm an address.
- Building a profile of a person beyond what one message needs. Do not collect
  their full work history, their location, or a list of all their accounts.
- Tracking signals, such as "last seen" times, activity history, or when a
  person is online.
- Linking an anonymous username to a real person by guessing.

## Data handling

- Collect the minimum: name, role, one or two pieces of evidence, one channel.
- Do not save information about the people outside the chat report, unless the
  user asks for a tracker. A tracker holds only: name, role, company, channel,
  date contacted, follow-up date.
- In the EU and UK, data protection law (GDPR, the General Data Protection
  Regulation) can apply to personal data, including work contact details. Whether
  a given message or record is allowed depends on the context. So keep to the
  minimum: send one relevant message, do not build or store profiles, and keep a
  tracker only if the user asks for one. This is general information, not legal
  advice.

## Contact limits

- One message per person per role. One follow-up after about 7 working days. Then
  stop.
- Do not contact more than two people at the same company for the same role at the
  same time. Several people get the same message and compare notes.
- If someone replies "no" or "please apply through the portal", respect it.

## When the user asks for more

If the user asks for something on the "not allowed" list, decline that part in one
or two calm sentences. Explain that it tends to hurt their chances. Then offer the
best professional channel you found.
